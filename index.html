ACT 01

The Eyes

INDORE // TIMELESS GAZE

B

Before there were highway sprints, independent apartments, or crowded coastal cafes, there was simply Chota Babu—the girl from Indore with the big, soulful eyes that could tell an entire story before she ever said a single word.

Those eyes carry warmth when she laughs, stubborn defiance when she’s annoyed, boundless tenderness when she cares, and a quiet stillness she rarely shows the world. People spend years trying to craft a presence; Sanjali simply looks at you, and the room feels grounded.

“Some things never change. From the earliest childhood snapshot to tonight in Indore, the gaze remains completely her own.”

ACT 02

The Leap

CHENNAI & PUNE // COURAGE IN MOTION
HERO: TRADITIONAL DAY (YELLOW KURTA)

Sanjali in the bright yellow kurta on stage, singing 'Lag Ja Gale'. Absolute showstopper.

+ Assign leap-hero
Lag ja gale ke phir ye haseen raat ho na ho...

She battles quiet underconfidence. She will tell you she gets nervous, that she doubts herself, that she lacks public speaking courage. But if you pay close attention to her life, a different pattern emerges: she gets scared, and then she does it anyway.

She left the familiar streets of Indore for Chennai, pivoting from mechanical engineering into the unknown world of IT. On day one, she walked straight up and offered a firm, confident handshake. On Traditional Day, hands slightly trembling, she took the microphone in a radiant yellow kurta and sang Lag Ja Gale so breathtakingly that the entire auditorium went quiet.

When a rented scooty arrived, she took the handlebars in a cute pink helmet and navigated Chennai traffic with laughter. When a Royal Enfield Bullet was parked in Pune, she climbed on, learned the clutch, and felt the exhilarating rush of riding a motorcycle herself. Courage was never the absence of fear—it was Sanjali doing it regardless.

The Palate

INDORE • CHENNAI • MUMBAI • THANE // FLAVOR TRAILS

Food has always been her compass. Indori poha and authentic sweets are the bedrock of her soul—flavors she measures the rest of India against.

In Chennai, survival meant trading the daily ₹45 meal voucher for dahi puri, sev puri, and samosa chaat just to feel a whisper of North Indian comfort. It meant the classic McVeggie at McDonald’s, ordering daily deliveries from EatClub when campus food failed, and hunting down paneer parathas at Golden Cafe.

Later, when corporate anxiety and the cold pressure of the "CTC world" crept in, she turned food into medicine: winding weekend trails for steaming ramen, slurping rich pho broth, comforting bowls of spaghetti, and fresh sushi.

And then came August 4th: her very first craft beer, unlocking a lifelong obsession with Mango Craft Beer. From Kadak Brewpub to Pop Tate's, from sweets at Prashant Corner in Thane to 2 AM roadside Chinese in Kopar Khairane and Kepchaki momos—every city she touched, she mapped in flavor.

ACT 05

The Heartbeat

LONAVALA // THE FIERCE CAPACITY TO LOVE
SACRED POLAROID: BABY JUJU

Kitten Juju looking sweet, curled up on the Lonavala villa road trip, or pampered at home.

+ Assign heartbeat-juju
Forever in the villa. Forever on the wallpaper.

She used to insist that she wasn’t an “animal person.” And then, on a busy road in early June, she saw a tiny, defenseless stray kitten alone on the asphalt. Without a second thought, she scooped her up, brought her home, bottle-fed her, named her Juju, and loved her with the fierce, protective devotion of a mother.

Juju wasn’t just a rescued kitten—she was royalty. Swaddled into bags for a private villa road trip in the misty hills of Lonavala, ruling the couches, pampered by everyone.

When little Juju was taken away too soon, Sanjali laid her to rest with her own bare hands. That wallpaper on her phone is not a monument to sorrow; it is proof of something sacred about Sanjali’s heart. When she loves, she loves with every single fiber. Completely, bravely, and without condition.

ACT 06

The Sisterhood

CHUNNA & MANSI // THE CIRCLE THAT HOLDS

In July 2025, the house in Indore felt hollow. Chunna had moved across the world to the US for her Master’s, and Mansi had packed her life for an MBA. It felt like her anchors had been scattered to the wind.

And then came December: Chunna flew home after an entire year, Mansi arrived, and the three of them were tearing up the go-karting tracks in Indore, laughing so hard the previous months of silence vanished into thin air.

In January, Mansi was in Mumbai—pre-gaming at Sanjali's flat, cracking up at Raunak's standup show, catching sunsets at Juhu Beach. In May, Mansi was in Pune—midnight scooty sprints through Viman Nagar, the girls ganging up to roast, playfully fighting over who gets to hold Sanjali's hand and sandwiching her right in the middle like a queen.

Even across oceans, Sanjali watches over Chunna like a guardian—refusing to let her sister compromise or settle for anything less than she deserves. These women are not just her people; they are the architecture of her strength.

ACT 08

The Quiet Strength

HONORING WHAT SHE CARRIED // 2025 – 2026
OPTIONAL: QUIET PORTRAIT

Contemplative, candid portrait in stillness (by a window, sunset, quiet moment).

+ Assign strength-portrait

This year demanded a great deal from her. She stepped out into the world alone at twenty-three. She absorbed the corporate pressures and salary expectations of the "CTC world" while still finding her footing as an independent woman.

When her mother fell ill in Chennai, she rushed to Indore. When her Nani fought terminal cancer, she traveled to Ujjain to hold her mother's hand. When her Nani passed on September 3rd, she stayed in Mumbai and kept reporting to work because that was her duty—bearing quiet sorrow for her grieving family while soldiering through corporate expectations.

She endured a toxic living situation with a former friend, held her dignity, and walked away with her head held high. She survived weeks of silence after losing Juju, learning to breathe through the dark.

“She didn’t just survive this year. She carried everyone else’s weight on her shoulders, protected her peace, and still found ways to laugh till her stomach hurt.”
ACT 09

The Unfinished Song

HER VOICE // WAITING TO BE UNPAUSED
HER VOICE: SINGING OR KARAOKE

Traditional Day singing, Bandra karaoke bar (RHB), or candid humming/music moment.

+ Assign song-stage
Your voice is too beautiful to keep to yourself.

In that yellow kurta in Chennai, she opened her mouth to sing Lag Ja Gale, and the entire hall stopped breathing. There is something delicate, textured, and deeply emotive about her voice—it doesn’t perform; it speaks directly to the soul.

In late February 2026, she took a brave step and launched her own singing page to share her gift. By late April, the relentless noise of corporate life and quiet imposter syndrome crept in, and she paused it.

Songs don’t disappear simply because you stop singing them. That artist inside her is still there, waiting for the corporate static to clear. This is a gentle, loving reminder on your birthday:

Unpause it.

From mechanical engineering to IT support, and now setting her sights on conquering the Product Manager world—she is sharpening her intellect, studying, and taking the helm of her own professional destiny.

She scouted apartments across Mumbai, took charge, and finalized her fresh start at Casa Rio. She built a neighborhood rhythm that belongs only to her: stepping out of Everest Dynamic Crest, peaceful morning walks by Khidkaleshwar Mandir, and strolls to Lodha Mall for pints of mango craft beer at Kadak Brewpub.

ACT 11

To Sanjali, On Your Birthday

NOVEMBER 3, 2026 // FROM THE EYEWITNESS

Dear Sanjalu,

If you look back across the last fifteen months, you might see exhausting commutes, long local trains, corporate tests, moving boxes, and moments where your heart felt too heavy to carry alone.

But from where I was standing, this is what I saw:

  • I saw a girl who landed alone in Chennai with quiet anxiety, and stepped onto a stage in a yellow kurta to sing Lag Ja Gale like an absolute dream.
  • I saw a girl who learned to ride motorcycles, conquered swimming pools, and dominated badminton courts without breaking a sweat.
  • I saw a girl who scooped up a helpless kitten from the roadside, gave her a queen’s love in Lonavala, and loved her with her whole soul.
  • I saw a girl who survived every difficult living space, fiercely protected her sister across the world, stood as an anchor for her mother, and closed her own new home in Casa Rio.
  • And I saw a girl who struts down Marine Drive looking so breathtaking that the entire city disappears around her.

You don't have to prove anything to the CTC rat race. You don't have to carry the weight of having every single answer figured out today. You are already an extraordinary, deeply loved, fiercely capable woman.

Happy Birthday, Chotu. I hope when you look in the mirror today in Indore, you finally see what everyone else has seen all along.

Always in your corner,
Mayank
